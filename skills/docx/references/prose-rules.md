# DOCX prose rules

This reference combines the Humanizer and antislop-copywriting constraints for Word documents.

## Preserve source fidelity

Keep all supported facts and remove unsupported filler. Never invent names, dates, numbers, citations, quotations, results, or product capabilities. Mark placeholders clearly. Do not rewrite quotations, titles, code, commands, URLs, Word field codes, or source data.

## Write for the reader

Choose the register from the document. Use plain, specific language for technical, academic, legal, and factual documents. Follow a user writing sample when one exists. Name the actor when the source gives one. Use passive voice only when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally omitted.

## Remove AI patterns

Cut staged openers, fake objections, empty signposting, inflated importance, aphorisms, generic conclusions, filler, stacked hedges, forced groups of three, false ranges, synonym cycling, repeated closers, decorative emoji, all-caps emphasis, excessive bold, scare quotes, and bold labels that repeat the sentence.

Use paragraphs for explanation. A list must represent a real sequence, set of requirements, options, or scan-friendly items. End sections on the last useful fact.

## Final review

1. Read the source and draft once for meaning.
2. Audit the draft for unsupported claims and AI patterns.
3. Read it aloud and revise for natural rhythm and specific wording.
4. Check authored prose for em and en dashes. Keep them only inside source quotations or technical syntax.
5. Confirm that heading hierarchy, captions, tables, and fields remain Word-native.
